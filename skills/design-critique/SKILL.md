# Design Critique

Judge the design that exists, not the design you wish had been requested.

## Workflow

1. Resolve the brief, audience, product job, and authority chain.
2. Inspect the strongest available evidence: artifact, render, screenshots, states, source, and project context.
3. Separate observation from interpretation. Cite exact visible or source evidence before giving a judgment.
4. Read [critique-rubric.md](references/critique-rubric.md) and evaluate only relevant dimensions. Use the [design parameter field guide](../design-router/references/design-parameters.md) and relevant domain references when diagnosing the specific relationships behind a strength or problem. Judge their fit to the brief; neither an unused parameter nor an expressive choice is a defect by itself.
5. Apply taste carefully: label evidence as project, contextual, or global. Never present a provisional preference as a law.
6. Prioritize the few changes that would materially improve the design. Preserve intentional features that serve the brief even when an automated convention dislikes them.

## Output

Lead with the overall diagnosis in one paragraph. Then provide a compact findings table with:

- priority
- evidence/location
- observation
- interpretation
- recommended change
- confidence

Include strengths only when they reveal what must be preserved. End with the smallest coherent refinement direction and any uncertainty that needs visual proof.

## Boundaries

- Read-only by default: no edits, installs, generated events, or context writes.
- Do not turn personal taste into accessibility language or vice versa.
- Do not reward novelty by itself. Distinctiveness must help the subject, use, or emotional contract.
- Do not use generic praise. If a feature works, say why and what would break it.
