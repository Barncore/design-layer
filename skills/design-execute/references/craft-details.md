# Details worth inspecting

Load the relevant section when the artifact calls for finer control. These are decision prompts, not a checklist of effects to add.

## Type and optical balance

Choose type by its behaviour in this content: x-height, width, aperture, contrast, terminals and rhythm. The same nominal size can produce different visual size. Compare real labels, numerals, punctuation and long text. Match fallback metrics where font loading would shift the composition. Use tabular figures for aligned changing data, not indiscriminately for prose.

Optical size, grade, width and weight are different controls in variable fonts. Optical size can change detail for a reading size; grade can alter darkness with less reflow where the font supports it. Use available axes for a reason. Inspect punctuation, icon centres and curved edges optically rather than trusting bounding boxes. Preserve the intended line breaks without hard-coding a fragile desktop layout.

## Space and composition

Spacing expresses relationships. Distinguish gaps within a unit, between units and between sections. One repeated gap everywhere weakens grouping. Choose density by region and task; a compact information area can sit inside a spacious composition. Consider whether alignment, proximity or a rule can group content before adding a container.

Inspect both occupied and empty shapes. Tension can come from asymmetry, crop, scale or an interrupted rhythm while the underlying reading order stays clear. Optical corrections may depart from the spacing scale. Judge whether they improve the actual composition rather than banning them as inconsistency.

## Colour and material

Judge colour in its surrounding area, quantity and role. Hue, perceived lightness and chroma answer different questions. A beautiful palette of equal swatches may fail when one colour occupies most of a screen. Check contrast in rendered states, including gradients, imagery and translucent layers. Colour-emotion claims are context dependent; do not promise a universal psychological effect.

Texture scale, roughness, blur, edge treatment and light direction can establish a material language. Keep controls legible and affordances understandable. A stylistic detector warning about glow, a font or a gradient is a question for judgment, not a ban.

## Interaction and motion

Make origin, destination and object continuity understandable. Consider interruption, reversal, velocity and repeated-use fatigue as well as duration. Preserve focus and selection when content transforms. Choose reduced-motion behaviour that preserves meaning, such as immediate replacement or a restrained dissolve, instead of removing the only state cue. Pointer play needs an equivalent usable touch and keyboard path. Sound is opt-in and must not carry essential information alone.

## State and content

Inspect the longest plausible title, empty collection, real image proportions, errors, undo, localisation and text zoom. Keep novelty in the intended experience while familiar controls remain interpretable. A staged discovery can be appropriate in a portfolio and frustrating in a frequent work tool. Test that distinction in context.
