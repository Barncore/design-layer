# Surface, light & depth

How a screen suggests paper, glass, ink, metal or no material at all.

Use these dimensions to make project-specific choices. The alternatives are examples to combine or extend, not scores or required opposites. Choose from the brief and inspect the consequences; the user does not need to answer each dimension.

## material-01: Material metaphor

**Alternatives:** None / paper / glass / metal / liquid

**What changes:** Materials imply different kinds of objects and behaviour.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose a material because its visual and behavioural properties serve the subject or experience; an intentionally flat interface is also a coherent choice.

## material-02: Surface grain

**Alternatives:** Clean / fine grain / coarse texture

**What changes:** Texture scale and contrast decide whether it is felt or visibly read.

**Apply to the brief:** Select grain scale and contrast for the intended viewing distance and display size; ensure texture does not erode text, image detail or motion performance.

## material-03: Light character

**Alternatives:** Diffuse / hard / luminous from within

**What changes:** Light direction and softness change perceived form.

**Apply to the brief:** Use light direction, softness and emitted light to describe form and hierarchy; maintain a coherent relationship among neighbouring surfaces.

## material-04: Translucency

**Alternatives:** Opaque / frosted / refractive

**What changes:** What remains visible behind a surface changes both depth and legibility.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose what should remain visible through a surface and why; verify readability across changing backdrops and provide a suitable opaque or reduced-effects state.

## material-05: Edge construction

**Alternatives:** Sharp / rounded / bevelled / irregular

**What changes:** Edges can describe a material or simply organise a control.

**Apply to the brief:** Use edge construction to express a material or distinguish component roles; coordinate nested shapes, hit areas and the actual clipping behaviour.

## material-06: Depth cue

**Alternatives:** Flat planes / shadow / perspective / parallax

**What changes:** Depth can establish a hierarchy or create an explorable scene.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose the least or most spatial treatment the intended experience needs; make depth relationships legible without requiring parallax or perspective everywhere.

## material-07: Imperfection rule

**Alternatives:** Exact repetition / bounded variation

**What changes:** A slightly irregular edge differs from arbitrary random damage.

**Apply to the brief:** Define where variation may occur and what remains stable so an imperfect family looks intentional rather than arbitrarily damaged.
