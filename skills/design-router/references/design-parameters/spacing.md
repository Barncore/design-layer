# Space & density

The distances that turn separate objects into relationships.

Use these dimensions to make project-specific choices. The alternatives are examples to combine or extend, not scores or required opposites. Choose from the brief and inspect the consequences; the user does not need to answer each dimension.

## spacing-01: Grouping distance

**Alternatives:** Nearly equal gaps / strong within-between contrast

**What changes:** Changing only the gap ratio can change what belongs together.

**Apply to the brief:** Set within-group gaps in relation to between-group gaps so the intended units are apparent; judge the ratio in the real layout, not as isolated token values.

## spacing-02: Density distribution

**Alternatives:** Uniform / dense islands / gradual release

**What changes:** A screen can be information-rich without making every region equally dense.

**Apply to the brief:** Allocate density by region and task: comparison may need a dense island while orientation or imagery benefits from open space.

## spacing-03: Vertical cadence

**Alternatives:** Even increments / phrase-like intervals

**What changes:** A page can move in repeated beats or vary with the content.

**Apply to the brief:** Let vertical intervals mark phrases, groups and sections; choose regularity or variation according to the content rhythm and scanning needs.

## spacing-04: Container dependence

**Alternatives:** Open alignment / rules / enclosed surfaces

**What changes:** Boxes are one way to group information, not the only way.

**Apply to the brief:** Try alignment, proximity or rules before deciding that enclosure is useful; use containers where they clarify ownership, state or interaction.

## spacing-05: Reading measure

**Alternatives:** Narrow column / broad line / multiple columns

**What changes:** Line length changes the shape and pace of a reading area.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose line length with type size, content depth and viewport; inspect actual paragraphs and compare reading comfort with the desired composition.

## spacing-06: Optical spacing

**Alternatives:** Mathematical equality / perceptual correction

**What changes:** Curves, punctuation and icons can look off-centre despite equal bounds.

**Apply to the brief:** Correct perceived alignment where curves, punctuation or icon mass defeat geometric centring; keep the correction responsive and locally justified.

## spacing-07: Whitespace role

**Alternatives:** Separation / anticipation / dramatic pause

**What changes:** A large gap can mark a section or deliberately slow an encounter.

**Apply to the brief:** Give a large gap a job such as separation, anticipation or a pause; check whether it improves the encounter or merely makes a routine task longer.
