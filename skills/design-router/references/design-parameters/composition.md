# Composition & tension

Where the eye lands, how a page holds together, and what breaks its rhythm.

Use these dimensions to make project-specific choices. The alternatives are examples to combine or extend, not scores or required opposites. Choose from the brief and inspect the consequences; the user does not need to answer each dimension.

## composition-01: Focal hierarchy

**Alternatives:** One dominant mass / distributed emphasis

**What changes:** Scale, area and contrast can compete or cooperate.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose the first thing the audience should understand; use one dominant mass for a clear entry point or distributed emphasis when comparison and discovery are the job.

## composition-02: Balance

**Alternatives:** Centred / weighted to an edge / diagonal

**What changes:** Symmetry can feel ceremonial; offset masses create tension. These are hypotheses to compare.

**Apply to the brief:** Place visual weight to support the intended reading order and character; test whether offset masses remain coherent across sizes instead of assigning symmetry a universal mood.

## composition-03: Grid visibility

**Alternatives:** Hidden structure / explicit rules / broken grid

**What changes:** An underlying grid can remain even when a few objects escape it.

**Apply to the brief:** Make structure visible when it helps scanning or expresses the subject; break it selectively where a meaningful exception benefits from the contrast.

## composition-04: Scale collision

**Alternatives:** Related sizes / extreme type-to-object contrast

**What changes:** A huge word and a tiny caption create a different reading tempo from a smooth hierarchy.

**Apply to the brief:** Use large size differences to establish importance or tension; check that supporting information remains legible and the hierarchy suits the project rather than merely performing drama.

## composition-05: Edge pressure

**Alternatives:** Generous inset / near-edge / full bleed

**What changes:** The distance to the frame changes whether a composition feels contained or expansive.

**Apply to the brief:** Decide which elements need containment and which imply a world beyond the frame; preserve useful reading insets and control reach at narrow sizes.

## composition-06: Cropping

**Alternatives:** Complete object / decisive cut / fragment

**What changes:** A cropped object suggests a larger world outside the screen.

**Apply to the brief:** Use fragments to direct attention or suggest scale, while retaining enough identity and context for the audience to understand the subject.

## composition-07: Overlap

**Alternatives:** Separate planes / interlocked masses

**What changes:** Overlap can connect type and image or interfere with reading.

**Apply to the brief:** Interlock type and image when their relationship carries the idea; check reading order, contrast and responsive collisions before accepting the overlap.

## composition-08: Rhythm

**Alternatives:** Regular repetition / syncopation / interruption

**What changes:** A repeated structure makes an exception more noticeable.

**Apply to the brief:** Establish a repeated beat, then let a content or narrative change motivate interruption; tune surprise to the intended encounter and frequency of use.

## composition-09: Figure and ground

**Alternatives:** Stable foreground / reversible reading

**What changes:** Negative space can become an active shape instead of unused room.

**Apply to the brief:** Use negative space as an active part of the composition when the secondary reading rewards attention without obscuring essential content.
