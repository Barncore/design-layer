# Type as a system

The small properties of letters and setting that create a voice.

Use these dimensions to make project-specific choices. The alternatives are examples to combine or extend, not scores or required opposites. Choose from the brief and inspect the consequences; the user does not need to answer each dimension.

## typography-01: Skeleton and proportion

**Alternatives:** Geometric / humanist / grotesque / calligraphic

**What changes:** Look at the shape of a, g, e and the width of common words.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose letter structures that fit the subject and reading context; inspect distinctive glyphs alongside ordinary words rather than relying on a font genre name.

## typography-02: Stroke contrast

**Alternatives:** Nearly uniform / sharp thick-thin contrast

**What changes:** The relation between thick and thin strokes affects texture and fragility.

**Apply to the brief:** Judge stroke contrast at the intended rendering size and background; let delicacy or force serve the voice without sacrificing reading.

## typography-03: Width and fit

**Alternatives:** Condensed / normal / extended

**What changes:** Width changes both voice and the architecture of a headline.

**Apply to the brief:** Use width to shape word rhythm and headline architecture; compare actual copy, wrapping and available font axes rather than horizontally distorting text by default.

## typography-04: Optical size

**Alternatives:** Text construction / display construction

**What changes:** Some fonts alter detail, spacing and proportions for reading size.

**Apply to the brief:** Use the font’s supported optical sizing to keep text useful at small sizes and permit display character at large sizes; verify actual glyph behaviour.

## typography-05: Terminals and ink traps

**Alternatives:** Clean endings / flared cuts / deep notches

**What changes:** Tiny contour decisions can become a signature at large sizes.

**Apply to the brief:** Let distinctive contour details carry identity where their size makes them meaningful; inspect small-size clarity before extending them to interface text.

## typography-06: Softness and irregularity

**Alternatives:** Crisp / swollen / deliberately wonky

**What changes:** Controlled imperfection can shift a voice without replacing the whole typeface.

**Apply to the brief:** Select the amount and location of irregularity for the intended voice; a warm headline and a precise data area can belong to the same system.

## typography-07: Tracking and case

**Alternatives:** Tight sentence case / open capitals / mixed emphasis

**What changes:** Spacing and case can transform a familiar font.

**Apply to the brief:** Use tracking and case to establish register and hierarchy; judge spacing with the actual font, words and reading size.

## typography-08: Line shape

**Alternatives:** Conventional wrapping / deliberate rag / stepped setting

**What changes:** The silhouette of the text block participates in composition.

**Apply to the brief:** Shape phrase breaks and the text silhouette when they contribute to meaning; keep that relationship resilient to copy changes, translation and narrow screens.

## typography-09: Role contrast

**Alternatives:** One adaptable family / contrasting families

**What changes:** Related forms can unify roles; a different voice can mark a change of register.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose related or contrasting type roles according to the change of register; compare the whole composition before adding another family.

## typography-10: Typographic behaviour

**Alternatives:** Static / variable axes / kinetic letterforms

**What changes:** Changing letterforms can express state or become the content itself.

**Apply to the brief:** Use kinetic or variable type when its behaviour communicates state or is part of the experience; retain readable text and an appropriate reduced-effects presentation.
