# Colour relationships

Context, quantity, lightness and the difference between association and effect.

Use these dimensions to make project-specific choices. The alternatives are examples to combine or extend, not scores or required opposites. Choose from the brief and inspect the consequences; the user does not need to answer each dimension.

## colour-01: Lightness structure

**Alternatives:** Close values / separated planes

**What changes:** Try the design in greyscale to see the hierarchy supplied by lightness.

**Apply to the brief:** Use lightness relationships to organise planes and emphasis; inspect greyscale as a diagnostic while measuring real text and control contrast separately.

## colour-02: Chroma distribution

**Alternatives:** Muted throughout / small intense accent / saturated field

**What changes:** Quantity changes the role of a colour.

**Apply to the brief:** Select colour intensity together with the area it occupies; test the palette in a complete composition before treating an attractive swatch as a surface colour.

## colour-03: Temperature relation

**Alternatives:** Warm / cool / warm-cool opposition

**What changes:** A warm element in a cool field can feel different from the same element alone.

**Apply to the brief:** Use temperature relationships to connect or differentiate regions; judge the same colour in its actual surroundings instead of assigning a fixed emotional effect.

## colour-04: Near-neighbour tension

**Alternatives:** Close hues / complementary contrast / offbeat neighbour

**What changes:** A slightly unexpected neighbour can be more distinctive than a textbook palette.

**Apply to the brief:** Explore hue relationships that express the project, including near neighbours; compare coherence and distinguishability rather than treating colour-wheel recipes as requirements.

## colour-05: Surrounding colour

**Alternatives:** Same swatch, different field

**What changes:** Perceived colour changes with its surrounding field even when the colour value stays constant.

**Apply to the brief:** Hold a colour constant while changing the surrounding field to distinguish a relationship effect from a change in the colour itself.

## colour-06: Gradient path

**Alternatives:** Hue sweep / lightness drift / layered field

**What changes:** Interpolation space and direction change the journey between endpoints.

**Apply to the brief:** Choose the gradient’s direction and interpolation path for its role in the composition; inspect intermediate colours, gamut and text over the resulting field.

## colour-07: Neutral bias

**Alternatives:** Paper / chalk / charcoal / tinted neutral

**What changes:** A neutral can carry warmth, coolness or a slight chromatic cast.

**Apply to the brief:** Select neutral bias in relation to imagery, materials and sustained reading; preserve surface distinctions and accessibility across themes.

## colour-08: Semantic palette

**Alternatives:** Mood colours / state colours / shared system

**What changes:** A favourite accent may also need to indicate selection, warning or success.

**Apply to the brief:** Map atmosphere and functional state colours deliberately so selection, warning and success remain understandable; keep semantic roles stable when theme values change.
