# Project intent interview

Use this when establishing the creative direction for a website or app UI. Assume the user has a developed vision for the project, product or brand, even when its visual expression is undecided. Read the existing brief, project material and conversation first. Carry forward established answers; scoped edits and approved directions do not need a fresh interview.

Ask about things the user can answer from that vision: what the project is trying to achieve, whom it serves, how people will encounter or use it, what distinguishes its approach, and how visitors should feel. Look for reasons and revealing details from which you can infer design choices. The user supplies knowledge of the project; you own the creative translation. Avoid asking them to invent a visual identity or prescribe a design they have not seen. Choose questions from the project rather than a fixed questionnaire.

Use three rounds as the normal shape for developing a new creative direction: discovery, refined discovery, then visual interpretation and resolution. Aim for 3–7 focused questions per round, with one main thing to answer in each. The range supports depth without turning the interview into homework. Do not manufacture questions to reach it, hide several questions inside one, or repeat supplied information. The visual discussion can be shorter when little remains. Continue from equivalent discovery and feedback already completed, and respect an explicit request to skip or shorten the interview.

## Round one: divergent discovery

Discover both the project's practical purpose and the material that could make its expression distinctive. Explore the audience's circumstances, the project's ambitions and beliefs, its particular approach, meaningful stories, tensions and intended experience. Follow interesting details that could generate creative possibilities, even when the basic requirements are clear. Resolving uncertainty is one reason to ask a question; opening a promising line of thought is another.

Digest the answers together. Consider how the underlying priorities and distinctive qualities could influence hierarchy, composition, typography, imagery, colour, interaction, vibe and motion. An individual adjective does not determine a visual treatment. Use the relevant domains of the [design parameter field guide](design-parameters.md) to develop that creative translation internally; keep interview questions about the project rather than asking the user to specify parameter settings.

Develop three promising interpretations internally. They should offer meaningfully different readings of the intended experience, rather than cosmetic variations on one idea. Treat them as provisional possibilities, not a closed menu. There is no need to present them to the user at this point.

## Round two: inference and refined discovery

Ask questions whose answers help distinguish, deepen or challenge those interpretations. Keep the questions grounded in the project so the user can answer from their knowledge, without having to choose a design prematurely. Explore what would change the interpretation, what an attractive idea might misunderstand, and whether a more interesting direction is emerging. Seek evidence against your current favourite as well as support for it. Let the answers replace the initial possibilities when warranted.

This round should still create possibilities. Do not turn it into confirmation of one preferred direction or a disguised choice between three predetermined styles. Share a concise understanding check if useful, while leaving room for the answers to change your interpretation.

## Round three: visual interpretation and resolution

After digesting round-two answers, open this round with an image using [visual-understanding.md](visual-understanding.md). Show the strongest current interpretation and briefly explain the consequential connections between the brief and what the image expresses. If two materially different readings remain plausible, a focused visual comparison can help clarify the choice. Respect requests to skip images and use the fallback guidance when image generation is unavailable.

Use the image to make subjective reactions easier to express. Ask focused questions that reveal what feels appropriate or misplaced, catch mistaken assumptions, and resolve choices that could materially change the experience. The user now has something concrete to react to. Let unexpected feedback reopen discovery when it changes the intent.

The image expresses your understanding; it is not a final website specification. Agreement with the intended experience does not approve every pictured layout, colour, typeface or component. Carry confirmed intent into design and keep incidental visual choices open unless the user selects them.

At the close, ask whether the user wants to start building, continue with another round of questions, or see a revised or alternative image. Recommend the useful next step and explain any consequential uncertainty. Infer whether another image should apply corrections or explore a different interpretation from the feedback; clarify that distinction only when needed. Let the user's answer determine how to continue. Do not cut useful discovery merely to save tokens; establishing direction can prevent substantial rework.

## Keep an evolving brief

Reuse the established project brief; otherwise maintain `DESIGN-BRIEF.md` at the project root. Update it after each answered round and later corrections. Keep the current intent, audience, priorities, desired experience, constraints, interpretations and open choices clear. Preserve the reasons and revealing examples behind decisions. Distinguish confirmed intent from provisional inference and mark replaced interpretations as superseded.

Record image locations, their role, and what the user confirmed or corrected. The brief must not turn a positive reaction into approval of incidental details or silently override project decisions. Keep one concise current account, with links to supporting material rather than a transcript or a record of internal deliberation. If the task explicitly forbids file writes, maintain the brief in the conversation.

Before building, check that the brief reflects the latest answers and visual feedback. Carry its location into the design handoff and read it when continuing work. Refine the direction through concrete design and the user's reactions.
